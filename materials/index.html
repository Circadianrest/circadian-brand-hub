What the certifications actually cover

Certification scope is the most misread thing in this category, so it is set out plainly. A certification that covers a cover fabric is not a certification of the fill inside it.

FillCertificationScope
Organic cottonGlobal Organic Textile Standard (GOTS) Certified Organic, license GOTS-10229The finished pillow: the cotton fill and the cover over it. Held to GOTS Version 7.0, renewed on an on-site audit every year
Organic woolGOTS Certified Organic, license GOTS-10229The finished pillow, fill and cover
Buckwheat hullsNone on the fillThe hulls are a natural agricultural byproduct with nothing added. Only the cotton cover is certified organic. The fill carries no organic certification and we do not claim one
KapokNone on the fillWild-harvested, not farmed, so organic certification does not apply. Described as natural kapok, never as organic
Dunlop latexOEKO-TEX Standard 100 Class 1, FSC certifiedTested for substances of concern at the strictest OEKO-TEX class, the one written for items for babies and toddlers
Read this before comparing. A pillow advertised under $60 claiming GOTS almost always certifies only the outer fabric. The question worth asking any brand is which components the licence covers, and the licence number that proves it.

Buckwheat: why ours is quieter

Most buckwheat pillows use roasted hulls, which come out as three-sided pyramids. Those edges are why the category has a reputation for being crunchy and loud against the face.

Our hulls go through an air-jet cleaning process that breaks them into single-sided polished pieces before they go into the cover. In our own testing this cuts the crunch by up to 68 percent, smooths the transitions when you shift position, and leaves the surface softer against the skin, while preserving the flex-and-lock support buckwheat is known for. The 68 percent figure is our measurement, not an independent laboratory result.

Adjusting the fill

Every Circadian pillow opens with a side zipper and arrives full, about a third more material than most people keep. Height is set at home, on the mattress that matters, rather than guessed at a factory.

Loose fills: cotton, kapok, buckwheat, shredded latex

Lift material out a handful at a time until the neck holds the same line it holds while standing. Keep what you remove; it goes back in whenever the mattress or the sleeper changes.

Wool

Wool arrives as sheets stacked in layers, not as loose fill. Peel a whole sheet off the outside of the stack and lay it back in any time. Never reach into the middle, and never tear or shred a sheet: a torn sheet cannot be undone, and the pillow then reads as broken rather than adjusted. A few hours in the sun relaxes and rebounds wool that has compressed.
