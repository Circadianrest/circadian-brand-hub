Staple length, ply and thread count as sixteen sheet brands publish them

Circadian · 2026-09-30

The variables that decide how a cotton sheet wears go largely unprinted on the pages that sell it. This entry records what sixteen sheet brands print about staple length, ply and thread count, and compares that against the list of variables a laboratory pilling test treats as relevant.

Scope and method

On 14 September 2026 we read the published product pages of sixteen sheet brands, ours included. Every value below is the brand's own wording, taken from its own page on that date. We tested, handled and slept on none of these sheets. A blank means the page did not say, and says nothing about the sheet itself.

This method has limits. A page can omit a spec the mill knows. A page can print two figures, and two did. Wording can change after the capture date.

What the pilling test says matters

ASTM D4970 is the laboratory pilling test. It mounts fabric on a Martindale tester, rubs it against itself under a set load, and grades the result from 5 (no pilling) down to 1. The factors it names as moving that grade:

Factor named in ASTM D4970 Printed by the brands in this sample
Fiber type and blend Yes, on all sixteen pages
Fiber dimensions (staple) A staple claim on ten; a length or named variety on two
Yarn and fabric construction (ply) Two of sixteen
Finish Not tallied in this read
Yarns per square inch (thread count) Not on the ASTM list; printed on ten

The asymmetry is the finding. The figure most often used to compare sheets is the one the test leaves out, and the construction variable it includes is the one almost nobody prints.

Counts

  • Pages with any staple claim: 10 of 16. Among the five direct-to-consumer sheet specialists in the set, 5 of 5.
  • Pages going past the bare word "long-staple": 2 (Avocado, extra long staple; Parachute, Egyptian). Riley Home adds "combed".
  • Pages stating ply: 2 (Avocado, Naturepedic).
  • Pages printing both a count and a staple claim: every one lands between 270 and 480.

Why ply is the gap that matters

Under a 2005 Federal Trade Commission staff opinion, a yarn counts once in thread count whether it is single or plied. Two sheets with the same printed count can therefore be built from different yarns. Ply is part of yarn construction, which is on the ASTM list. A shopper comparing counts is comparing a number while the construction under it goes unstated.

What a more exact staple claim looks like

The word "long-staple" carries no length. A longer fiber spins into a yarn with fewer ends to work loose, so the sleeper gets a surface that stays smooth longer, and a named length or variety is the claim that lets you check it. Terms that commit a brand to something narrower:

Term What it pins down
Extra-long staple A length class beyond long-staple
Egyptian, Supima, Pima A named cotton variety
Combed Short fibers pulled out before spinning

Six of the sixteen pages print none of the fiber detail above, so a buyer has only the fiber type to go on.

What this entry does not tell you

  • Whether any listed sheet pills more or less than another. No fabric was graded.
  • Whether an unpublished spec is good or bad. Absence on a page is absence of disclosure only.
  • Anything about finish, which ASTM D4970 lists and this read did not tally.
