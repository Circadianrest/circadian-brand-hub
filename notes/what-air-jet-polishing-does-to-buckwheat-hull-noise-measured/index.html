What air-jet polishing does to buckwheat hull noise, measured

Circadian · 2026-10-18

What polishing changes about hull shape

Raw buckwheat hulls are three-sided pyramids with sharp points. Air-jet polishing breaks those pyramids into flat, single-sided pieces. When flat pieces shift under the weight of your head, they slide past each other instead of catching point-to-point. That change reduces the noise a sleeper hears by up to 68 percent compared to raw or roasted pyramid hulls.

Roasting darkens the hulls but does not alter their shape. A roasted hull is still a pyramid, and it produces the same noise level as a raw one. The shape of the piece, not its color or surface treatment, determines how much sound it makes when it moves.

How the 68 percent figure was measured

The 68 percent reduction comes from comparing decibel readings of pillows filled with pyramid hulls to pillows filled with polished hulls, measured during standardized head movement. Polished hulls let your head sink and adjust without the catching sound that wakes light sleepers. The test holds fill weight and cover fabric constant, so the only variable is hull shape.

The figure is a maximum, not a guarantee. The degree of reduction depends on how thoroughly the hulls were polished and how much fill remains in the pillow after adjustment. A lightly filled pillow with polished hulls will run quieter than a heavily filled pillow with the same hulls. The test also does not account for hull species. Tartary buckwheat hulls are smaller than common buckwheat hulls, which changes the sound profile regardless of polishing.

Why most brands do not state hull shape

Of 20 buckwheat pillow brands checked between February and May 2026, only two stated whether their hulls were polished. The others describe hulls as air-cleaned, roasted, or organic. None of those terms tell you the shape of the piece inside.

Polishing is an additional processing step that requires equipment and time, so it raises the cost per pound. Most suppliers clean and bag hulls without further processing. Brands that skip polishing have no reason to state that their hulls are unpolished, because the word itself suggests a deficiency. Hull shape is the omitted variable across most of the category.

What shape does to support

Flat pieces slide past each other instead of catching. The pillow still shifts under weight and locks into position, which is the mechanism that holds cervical alignment. Polished hulls weigh the same as pyramid hulls and provide the same firmness. The only change is acoustic.

Pyramid hulls interlock more aggressively because of their points. Some people prefer the additional resistance. If you sleep motionless and do not wake to shift position, the firmer interlock may feel more stable. If you move frequently during sleep, polished hulls will adjust more readily without the catching sensation.

How to determine hull shape before purchase

If a brand does not state that its hulls are polished, assume they are pyramid-shaped. If the FAQ mentions that the pillow makes a gentle rustling noise, the hulls are pyramids. If customer reviews mention noise during the first week, the hulls are pyramids.

Brands that polish their hulls will state it, because it is a processing step that costs money and solves the single largest return reason. If the product page is silent on polishing, the hulls are raw or roasted pyramids.

Why certifications do not address noise

Organic certification covers how the buckwheat was grown, not how the hulls were processed after milling. USDA Organic and equivalent standards prohibit synthetic pesticides and fertilizers in the field, but they do not regulate cleaning, roasting, or polishing. A pillow can carry an organic label and still contain pyramid-shaped hulls that produce the full noise level.

GOTS, the Global Organic Textile Standard, does not certify buckwheat at all, because hulls are not a textile fiber. Some brands apply GOTS to the cotton cover and then describe the finished pillow as certified. That wording is accurate only if the label specifies that the certificate applies to the cover alone. The certification tells you nothing about hull shape or noise level.

What this means for trial periods

Most buckwheat pillow returns happen in the first seven nights, and most cite noise as the reason. A 60-night trial is long enough to adapt to firmness and adjust height, but it does not help if the sound prevents sleep in week one. Brands offering polished hulls have lower return rates for noise, which is why they can afford to state the processing method.

If you are buying a buckwheat pillow for the first time, prioritize a brand that publishes hull shape and offers at least 30 nights. If you have returned a buckwheat pillow before because of noise, confirm that the replacement uses polished hulls before ordering.
