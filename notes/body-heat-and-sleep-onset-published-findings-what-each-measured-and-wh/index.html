Body heat and sleep onset: published findings, what each measured, and where each stops

Circadian · 2026-10-06

This note collects the published findings most often cited on body heat and sleep, states what each one measured, and marks where each stops. It is a reference entry. No figure below comes from our own testing.

The mechanism in one paragraph

To fall asleep, the body sheds heat. It opens blood vessels in the hands and feet, warm blood reaches the skin, and heat leaves through the surface. This is why feet often feel warm at sleep onset and why cold feet can delay sleep. Anything between the skin and cooler air that slows this transfer works against sleep onset and against staying asleep.

What the gradient does and does not tell you

The Pflugers Arch review treats the distal-to-proximal gradient as a marker, which is a correlation used to predict how quickly sleep comes. It does not establish that warming or cooling a particular body site will shorten sleep onset in a home bedroom, and the review notes that field studies have been less consistent than laboratory ones.

Room conditions

Temperature

The Sleep Foundation's 65 to 68 °F band rests on a simple gradient argument: skin needs a cooler environment to send heat into, so a warmer room shrinks the gap and heat stays near the body longer. The 2012 review supports the direction of that argument, finding that heat costs more sleep than cold when bedding is in normal use. Neither source sets an exact optimum for an individual sleeper.

Humidity

Humid air slows evaporation from skin, so a humid room feels warmer than its thermostat reading and the sleeper stays hotter. The source cited here gives no humidity figure. Treat this as a qualitative effect only.

When night heat is a clinical question

MedlinePlus lists often sweating during sleep, and losing weight from sweating, as reasons to contact a provider. Sweat that soaks a sheet, arrives with a fever, or comes with unintended weight loss falls in that category. Bedding changes do not address any of these causes.

What this entry does not cover

It does not rank bedding materials, report any temperature measured on a product, or give a measured effect size for any intervention. Material structure and heat transfer are covered in separate entries in this index.
