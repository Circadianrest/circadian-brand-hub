Pillow size labels and the inches behind them: standard, queen, king and body

Circadian · 2026-10-03

Pillow size names read like measurements and are not. "Body", "king" and "standard" are labels that each maker fills with its own inches. This entry lists the dimensions that sit behind those labels, where each figure comes from, and the cases where a label stops predicting fit.

Size labels and the inches behind them

Every width in the table is 20 inches. Length is the only axis that changes, and it changes a lot.

Gaps between labels

Comparison Length difference (in)
King pillow vs market body pillow (36 vs 54) 18
Market body pillow vs a 72 inch body pillow (54 vs 72) 18
King pillow vs a 72 inch body pillow (36 vs 72) 36

The two most common body lengths sit 18 inches apart. So a case bought against the word "body" with no number beside it is a guess, and a wrong guess leaves a foot and a half of pillow uncovered.

Why a label cannot carry the number

"Body size" commonly means 20 x 54, but the exact cut and seam allowance vary by maker. Two cases both sold as 20 x 54 can differ in how they sit on the same pillow. A case sewn for 54 inches also has no spare fabric to stretch over another 18. Length changes the pattern the case is cut from, and the fill volume inside does not change that.

A king case cannot stand in for a body case either. Both are 20 inches wide, and the king runs 18 inches shorter than even the market body length.

Measurement method

The only figure that settles fit is taken from the pillow itself.

  1. Fluff the pillow fully. A pillow pressed flat reads long and wide.
  2. Measure seam to seam along the length, then across the width.
  3. Buy a case cut to those two numbers, not to a size name.

When replacing a worn case, measure the pillow under it rather than reordering the old size from memory. The pillow is the fixed point, and every case option gets checked against it.

Why re-measuring matters over time

Natural fills do not hold a fixed length forever. Cotton and kapok can settle unevenly along a 72 inch pillow, so a case that fit on arrival can sit half an inch snug or loose a year later. When a case starts to feel wrong, measure the pillow again. The receipt and the original listing describe the pillow as it left the maker.

Where this reference stops applying

Situation Why the table above does not hold
Wedge or C-shaped pregnancy pillows These use their own sizing and case systems
Secondhand pillow with the tag removed No label to read, so hand measurement is the only source
Shopping outside the United States Labeling conventions and common lengths vary by region
A listing with a size name and no inches Treat the missing number as a reason to look further

What these figures do not tell you

  • They describe length and width only. Loft varies with fill and with how much fill a zippered pillow holds.
  • The Sleep Foundation figures describe the market in general, and any single brand can cut outside them.
  • The 72 inch figure is approximate, as published. Hand-filled pillows are made up and weighed one at a time, and a measured pillow is the better number.
