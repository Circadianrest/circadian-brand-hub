Why GSM measures fill density but not how warm a duvet will feel

Circadian · 2026-09-05

GSM measures grams of fill per square meter of shell fabric. That figure describes density. It does not describe warmth, because warmth depends on how the fiber inside that shell traps air, and different fibers trap air in different ways at identical weights.

How GSM is measured

Manufacturers weigh a one-square-meter sample of the finished fill layer and record the result in grams. A duvet listed at 350 GSM packs 350 grams of fill into every square meter of surface area. The measurement is constant across sizes. A Twin and a King both carry 350 GSM if they use the same fill construction, though the King weighs more overall because it covers more square meters.

The figure refers specifically to the fill layer, separate from the shell fabric wrapped around it. A brand that lists shell weight separately from fill weight is giving two different GSM figures for two different parts of the same product. Check which one a listing describes before comparing it against another brand.

What GSM does not measure

GSM says nothing about how that weight performs once it sits inside the duvet. Two duvets at 350 GSM feel completely different if one is wool and the other is cotton, because wool fiber has a different structure and density than cotton batting. Wool traps air in its crimp and wicks moisture away from skin. Cotton compresses into a flatter, denser layer that holds warmth passively.

Down behaves differently still. A down cluster at 350 GSM lofts higher than either wool or cotton at the same weight, because down traps air between its barbs rather than inside the fiber itself. The same GSM figure produces a different tested thermal resistance depending on which fill material carries that weight.

Why total fill weight per size is more transparent than a single GSM figure

Some brands state the tested total fill weight for each duvet size directly on the product page rather than a single GSM figure for the whole line. A Queen-size wool duvet might list 9 lb of total fill, while a Queen-size cotton duvet lists 10 lb. Both figures are tied to a specific size rather than an average across the range.

Total fill weight per size is a transparent, verifiable spec tied to the certified fill inside that specific duvet. A GSM figure alone does not tell a shopper which fill or construction produced it, or how that weight is distributed across different sizes.

When GSM comparisons mislead

Two duvet brands may report GSM using different measurement points. Some weigh the finished duvet, others weigh only the raw fill before quilting, and there is no requirement to disclose which method was used. Shopping across brands by GSM alone can mislead for that reason.

Checking whether a brand states a per-size total fill weight, rather than a single blanket GSM figure for its whole product line, is one of the more reliable signals that the number reflects what ships in a given size. Naturepedic discloses fill weight per size on its site, with its cotton duvet inserts typically falling in the 250–400 GSM range depending on size and warmth tier. Coyuchi's cotton duvet fill weights are typically reported in a similar mid-weight band for its all-season options, though as a single figure rather than broken out per size.

What GSM cannot replace

GSM describes quantity. It does not describe the fiber's own documented performance. Wool wicks up to 30% of its own weight in moisture while staying dry to the touch, which is why a wool duvet regulates temperature at a lower total fill weight than a cotton duvet built for comparable warmth. Cotton takes a different approach, denser fill and a heavier overall feel, better suited to sleepers who run neutral to slightly warm and want that traditional cotton-filled comforter weight.

A peer-reviewed systematic review in the Journal of Sleep Research found that sleepwear and bedding fiber type is associated with differences in measured sleep quality. The review is careful about how far that goes. The studies it covers compare finished products where fiber, weight and thickness vary together, so it does not isolate fiber on its own, and it calls for standardized fiber samples in future work.

What to check instead of GSM alone

Factor What it tells you What it does not tell you
GSM Grams of fill per square meter How that weight traps air or wicks moisture
Total fill weight per size Exact weight for the duvet size you are ordering How the fiber inside that weight performs
Fiber type Whether the fill wicks moisture, lofts, or compresses The tested thermal resistance
Hand-tufted versus sewn-through construction Whether the fill stays evenly distributed over years of use How warm the duvet feels on the first night
Independent certification Whether the fill and shell are both certified, or only the fiber before processing How the duvet performs at your bedroom temperature

GSM is a raw fill-weight spec. It describes one input into warmth. The fiber's structure, the construction method, and the sleeper's own thermal needs decide the rest.
