Indoor air guidance values for compounds emitted by flexible polyurethane foam

Circadian · 2026-10-15

The compounds that come off foam while your face is inches away

A pillow sits warm under your head for eight hours. Your face is a few inches from the material. The air above it is what you breathe all night. So it is fair to ask what the material gives off, and there is published work on it.

A 2018 paper in the EXCLI Journal derived indoor air guidance values for six compounds that come off flexible polyurethane foam. It had to derive them because none existed. Several are marked tentative, because the data has gaps.

A 2019 chamber study of eight polyurethane mattresses found that heat drove emission more than humidity or carbon dioxide did. Body heat is the one thing a sleeper cannot take out of the bed.

The counter-finding

A 2022 study in Chemosphere measured memory foam mattresses and concluded that the emissions are unlikely to put a consumer at risk. The literature does not agree with itself. Anyone who tells you it settles this in either direction has not read it.

What alternatives exist

Buckwheat hulls, wool, kapok, organic cotton and tree-tapped latex contain no polyurethane. The hulls are cleaned with air and UV light, so there is no fragrance or chemical finish to off-gas. Wool and cotton are certified organic under GOTS. Tree-tapped latex is washed and vulcanized with heat alone. If you would rather not referee a chemistry argument in your own bedroom, you can decline to have one.

How materials were processed, by fill type

What the market publishes here is thin. We read the published pages of 12 kapok pillow brands between February and May 2026. 12 of the 12 say anything at all about how the material was processed.

Brands publishing anything about processing: buckwheat 20 of 20, wool 19 of 19, kapok 12 of 12, organic cotton 10 of 10, natural latex 21 of 21, buckwheat and wool hybrid 3 of 6.

What a brand leaves out is what a buyer cannot check.

How this was measured

A desk study. The 2018 paper derived guidance values from available toxicological data. The 2019 chamber study placed polyurethane mattresses in sealed test chambers and varied temperature, humidity and carbon dioxide concentration to isolate which factor drove emission. The 2022 study measured actual memory foam mattresses and compared the results to health-based screening levels.

All three are published, peer-reviewed, and open to read. The 2022 paper is the only one of the three whose conclusion runs the other way.

What this does not tell you

These are chamber studies, not bedroom measurements. A chamber holds steady conditions that a bedroom never does. Air exchange rates, room volume, and the number of foam items in the space all change the exposure.

The 2022 paper's conclusion about low consumer risk rests on assumptions about ventilation and total foam mass that may not match your room.
