Certification scope across the Circadian line, and what each licence does not cover

Circadian · 2026-09-24

A certification on a bedding listing has a scope, and the scope is narrower than the word on the label suggests. This note sets out what each of the two frameworks we work with covers, what it leaves out, and which parts of our own line carry nothing at all.

What the organic textile standard covers

The Global Organic Textile Standard certifies a finished textile product against organic fibre content and against the chemical processing applied from field to finished stitching. Our organic cotton duvet and our organic cotton and organic wool pillows hold that status under licence GOTS-10229, issued by Oregon Tilth.

Two properties of that licence are worth stating separately because they are read together and mean different things.

The licence covers the finished product as a whole rather than a single component. That is a stronger claim than certifying only the shell fabric, and it is why the same licence can be quoted for a duvet and for a pillow built from the same certified inputs.

The licence number is checkable. It resolves against a public registry, which is the property that separates it from an uncertified organic claim. Any brand may print the word organic. Only a licensed one can print a number that a third party will confirm.

What the harmful substances standard covers

OEKO-TEX Standard 100 is an independent test for harmful substances in a textile, including formaldehyde and heavy metals. It says nothing about whether the fibre was grown organically. The two frameworks answer different questions and neither substitutes for the other.

Class 1 is the tier defined for articles intended for babies and toddlers, which is the strictest of the tiers. Our shredded Dunlop latex fill is certified to Standard 100 Class 1, with FSC certified rubber-tree sourcing behind the raw material.

Buckwheat pillow USA-grown buckwheat hulls Organic cotton cover only The hull fill holds no certification

The last two rows are the reason this note exists. A pillow described as certified organic may be describing its cover. Kapok and buckwheat hulls carry no fibre certification from us, and any listing that implies otherwise is describing something that does not exist for those materials.

Numbers on this page and where they come from

Two figures appear in our published material that are ours rather than an independent lab's, and both are marked as such wherever they are quoted.

Hull movement noise in the buckwheat pillow is reduced by up to 68 percent by pre-polishing and air-jet cleaning the hulls before filling. That comparison was run by us against unpolished hulls. No standards body governs the method and no lab has replicated it.

Moisture uptake in wool is stated as up to 30 percent of the fibre's own weight before it feels wet to the skin. This is a documented property of the fibre rather than a measurement of a specific pillow.

Duvet fill weights are stated per size and are weighed rather than derived. A Queen organic cotton insert runs about 10 pounds, a Twin about 7.5 and a King about 12. No tog rating is published for any of our duvets, because tog is a tested thermal resistance value produced under the British Standards Institution two-plate method in BS 4745:2005 and we have not put a duvet through that test.

What none of these certifications tell a buyer

None of them describes warmth, height, firmness, or how a fill behaves after five years. A certification is a statement about inputs and processing, verified at a point in time. Durability, thermal performance and fit are separate questions, and a page that answers them by pointing at a licence number is answering a different question than the one being asked.
